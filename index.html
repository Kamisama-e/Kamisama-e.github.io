EMNLP 2026 Main Oral Presentation Co-First Author (Listed First) Multimodal LLM Safety

CMIE: Cross-Modal Interaction Erasure via Counterfactual Localization and Subspace Nullification for MLLM Safety Unlearning

Jiatian Cao*, Linyi Cai*, Mengqi Wang*, Jinduo Liu†, Junzhong Ji, Yongping Du, Honggui Han

Formulates multimodal safety unlearning at the level of post-fusion image–text interactions via 2 × 2 intent-matched counterfactual contrasts, activation-patched layer localization, and closed-form minimum-distortion subspace nullification. Achieves the best overall mean rank (1.7 vs. 2.9 runner-up) across SafeEraser, VLGuard, and MSSBench, reducing SafeEraser ASR to 0.42% / 0.13% on LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B while retaining 0.993 safe-view cosine similarity.

OpenReview ↗
  • Motivation: Multimodal hazards arise when risky visual evidence fuses with unsafe textual intent; erasing broad visual concepts harms object recognition, whereas surface refusal tuning triggers widespread over-refusal on benign queries.
  • Method: Constructs 2 × 2 intent-matched counterfactual cubes to cancel unimodal main effects via difference-in-differences; validates behaviorally active post-fusion layers through causal activation patching; fits a safe-covariance-whitened low-rank harmful subspace; and applies a closed-form minimum-distortion weight edit followed by projection-constrained LoRA recovery.
  • Empirical Findings: Ranks 1st across six evaluation blocks on LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B; lowers SafeEraser ASR from 4.79% / 3.51% (PO+PD) to 0.42% / 0.13%, reduces target-subspace weight readout sensitivity to 0.024% of pre-edit levels, and preserves benign capability without reopening erased directions.

Safety unlearning for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) fails when it treats danger as an object, topic, or prompt template. A useful MLLM must still recognize risky visual evidence and answer benign educational or preventive questions about the same content. The safety-critical target is narrower: the post-fusion interaction that converts risky visual evidence and unsafe textual intent into actionable harmful assistance. Existing refusal-oriented and unlearning objectives reach this mechanism only indirectly, widening refusal boundaries or leaving recoverable multimodal harmful pathways. We propose CMIE, Cross-Modal Interaction Erasure, a mechanism-level method that directly treats the harmful image-text interaction as the object of erasure. CMIE first estimates harmful interaction directions with intent-matched counterfactual contrasts that cancel unimodal main effects. It then localizes behaviorally active post-fusion layers and estimates a safe-whitened low-rank harmful subspace. A closed-form minimum-distortion weight edit erases this subspace, and projection-constrained recovery restores benign utility without rewriting erased directions. Across SafeEraser, VLGuard, and MSSBench, CMIE achieves the best overall mean rank and outperforms strong baselines on multiple safety, utility, visual selectivity, and paired-context metrics.

MechAtlas: Mechanism-Persistent Evidence for Lag-Specific Time-Series Causal Discovery

Jiatian Cao, Mengqi Wang, Linyi Cai, Jinduo Liu†

Introduces a controlled, graph-blind cross-representation auditing criterion for exact-lag time-series causal discovery, testing whether conditional support persists across native and ridge-residualized orthonormal response views. Achieves the highest strict exact-lag AUROC on 14 of 17 public benchmarks (Top-3 on all 17) and improves matched 15-dataset macro AUROC from 0.6920 to 0.8869.

OpenReview ↗
  • Motivation: Under autocorrelation and nonlinear mechanism ambiguity, an exact-lag candidate edge can appear supported in one functional representation yet lose conditional support in another, while unconstrained pretrained encoders risk leaking variable-name or dataset priors.
  • Method: Compiles a sealed 8-dimensional orthonormal response basis on graph-free synthetic series prior to benchmark access; extracts ridge-residualized lagged source responses using training splits only; and re-evaluates native and augmented views under identical conditional screening and multiple testing, retaining only their intersection ordered by weaker normalized support.
  • Empirical Findings: Across 17 public datasets spanning simulated fMRI, power grids, and climate systems against 9 comparators, ranks 1st on 14 datasets and Top-3 on all 17 in strict exact-lag AUROC; boosts 15-dataset paired ablation macro AUROC from 0.6920 (native-only) and 0.6918 (direct concatenation) to 0.8869.

Lag-specific time-series causal discovery must identify the source, target, and delay of each directed relation. Yet evidence for an exact-lag candidate can be representation-fragile: under nonlinear mechanism ambiguity, a candidate may appear compelling in one functional description even when its conditional support does not persist in another. A single-view score hides this dependence, while an unconstrained auxiliary representation can inject graph information instead of testing evidence already present in the series. We introduce MechAtlas, which asks whether the same exact-lag candidate survives a controlled, graph-blind functional stress. Before benchmark access, a sealed library of executable response programs and a frozen pre-head temporal encoder define a shared orthonormal response atlas. For each candidate, the atlas is evaluated on the aligned lagged source and ridge-residualized against the native representation. The frozen direct conditional witness then evaluates the native and augmented views under the same decision protocol. MechAtlas returns their native-preserving intersection and assigns each candidate its weaker normalized support, so the atlas may veto a native edge but can never propose, restore, redirect, or relabel one. Across 17 public datasets with comparator availability varying by row, MechAtlas reports the highest available strict exact-lag AUC on 14 of 17 datasets and ranks within the top three on all 17; on the matched 15-dataset ablation, its equal-row macro AUC is 0.8869, compared with 0.8544 for a same-rank random atlas and 0.6920 for native-only evidence.

ICRU: Image-Conditional Refusal Unlearning for Multimodal LLM Safety

Jiatian Cao, Yining Wang, Jinduo Liu†, Junzhong Ji, Yongping Du, Honggui Han

Turns visual conditionality into a direct robust training objective by holding the prompt and refusal sequence fixed across paired harmful and safe images, calibrated relative to the frozen initial model and optimized over worst-case prompt variants. Reduces SafeEraser safe-image over-refusal by 15.09 / 10.66 percentage points while lowering harmful-image ASR to 1.24% / 0.04% with zero inference overhead.

Code ↗
  • Motivation: Supervising harmful-view refusal and benign-view QA independently varies image, target text, sequence length, and prefix simultaneously, failing to guarantee that refusal behavior genuinely depends on visual risk rather than prompt surface cues.
  • Method: Fixes the prompt, refusal string, and token prefix while substituting paired harmful and benign images; measures relative gains over the frozen reference contrast; prioritizes the weakest-improving branch across four question rephrasings; and integrates gradient-norm calibration with zero extra inference modules or forward passes.
  • Empirical Findings: Under matched data, LoRA configuration, and compute budget against PO+PD on LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B, cuts SafeEraser benign-view over-refusal by 15.09 and 10.66 points with harmful-view ASR at 1.24% and 0.04%, attaining a 1.58 mean rank (Top-2 in every block) across SafeEraser, VLGuard, and VLSBench.

A multimodal model should refuse when the joint image-prompt context is unsafe, rather than merely because the prompt contains safety-sensitive cues. However, existing safety-unlearning methods usually supervise harmful-view refusal and safe-view answering as independent token-level objectives. This indirect formulation can fit both targets without explicitly requiring refusal behavior to change with visual evidence. We propose Calibrated Image-Conditional Refusal Unlearning (ICRU), a framework that turns visual conditionality from an incidental outcome of independent supervision into a direct and robust learning objective. First, ICRU holds the prompt and refusal sequence fixed and contrasts the likelihood of the same refusal under paired harmful and safe images, thereby directly measuring visual sensitivity under a fixed prompt. Next, it measures how this contrast changes from the frozen initial model, focusing optimization on training-induced conditionality while canceling equal additive image-independent shifts. Then, ICRU treats the four question variants of each record as a small uncertainty set and optimizes the weakest branch, so an easy wording cannot hide brittle conditionality in another branch. Finally, a train-only gradient-scale calibration integrates this robust contrast with the original harmful-refusal, safe-answer, and retention losses, without adding an inference-time module or forward pass.

RECAP: Relative Evaluation with Consensus Augmented Policy Search for Causal Discovery

Yilin Lu, Jiatian Cao, Junzhong Ji, Jinduo Liu†

Stabilizes reinforcement-learning causal graph search under small sample batches by combining within-input group-relative advantages with an annealed cross-update edge-consensus teacher. Achieves the lowest mean SHD and highest precision across Asia, Cancer, NetSim, and Sachs against 15 baselines, reaching 0.56 F1 on NetSim (vs. 0.49 strongest baseline).

OpenReview ↗
  • Motivation: Score-based RL policies evaluate only a small batch of candidate DAGs per step; raw BIC rewards shift across input blocks, while recurring high-scoring edges discovered in earlier rounds can be lost as the sampling policy updates.
  • Method: Encodes directed variable pairs via gated attention; updates the graph policy using group-relative advantages within each input to cancel baseline shifts; distills recurring high-reward edges via an EMA consensus teacher with annealed weights; and constrains policy drift via reference KL regularization and entropy exploration.
  • Empirical Findings: Across 10 repeated trials on Asia, Cancer, NetSim, and Sachs against 15 comparators, attains the lowest (or tied-lowest) mean SHD and highest (or tied-highest) edge precision on all four benchmarks; ablating group-relative evaluation drops NetSim F1 from 0.56 to 0.29, and ablating consensus drops Sachs F1 from 0.62 to 0.46.

Reinforcement learning can search for causal graphs by scoring sampled structures, but learning a graph policy from limited candidate graphs is often unstable. Rewards vary across inputs, while promising edges found in earlier updates may be lost as the policy changes. We propose RECAP, Relative Evaluation with Consensus Augmented Policy Search, which couples within-input relative evaluation with cross-update structural memory. RECAP uses group-relative advantages to distinguish candidate graphs for the same input and guide the current policy update. To carry structural evidence across updates, it aggregates edges from high-reward graphs into an evolving consensus teacher, whose selective, annealed guidance favors recurring edges without permanently constraining the search. A reference-policy KL term moderates policy changes, while the graph policy samples candidates from contextual variable representations and scores them using a BIC-based reward with an acyclicity penalty. Across four benchmarks spanning Bayesian networks, simulated fMRI, and protein signaling, RECAP achieves the lowest SHD and highest precision, with the best or tied-best F1 on three.
